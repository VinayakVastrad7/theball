## 115 Island Cities and One Very Ambitious Billionaire

Jeff Effstein has reportedly unveiled plans to build 115 island cities, with the ultimate goal of creating one of the most powerful football leagues in the world.

While most people would worry about funding, infrastructure or whether anyone actually wants to live on these islands, Effstein has a bigger vision.

He wants a football ecosystem where billionaires can pump unlimited money into their clubs, sign the best players in the world and win trophies without having to explain their finances to regulators.

The idea is simple: every island gets a club, every club gets a billionaire, and nobody asks too many questions.

Effstein reportedly believes modern football has become unnecessarily complicated by financial restrictions and independent investigations. His proposal is to combine island cities with football clubs and let the market do whatever it wants.

## Financial Fair Play, Reimagined

Under Effstein's proposed structure, clubs would be free to receive billions from their owners. The real innovation, however, would be making all that spending look perfectly normal.

Clubs could sign sponsorship deals with companies owned by their investors, establish complicated commercial agreements and find creative ways to make their finances look respectable.

One club could receive a $900 million shirt sponsorship from a company whose entire business consists of selling three varieties of the same shirt.

Another could sign a player for €51 million on paper, then allegedly pay more than $200 million through agent fees, shell accounts and other arrangements conveniently buried in the paperwork.

When asked whether these valuations made any sense, a representative reportedly replied, “We don't care. We just hope everyone gets to sportswash their organisation.”

The market, however, could not be reached for comment.

## Regulators Will Get Their Own Islands

Effstein also reportedly plans to establish an independent authority to oversee the league, complete with investigators, financial experts and a committee responsible for enforcing the rules.

There is just one small detail: the authority would receive generous funding from the same billionaires whose clubs it is supposed to regulate.

Effstein's representatives insist this would create no conflict of interest.

“We believe the best way to regulate billionaires is to make sure the regulators understand their financial interests,” one league executive reportedly explained.

There are also allegations that club owners have discussed payments to officials, referees and other influential individuals to protect their interests.

No evidence has been established within the proposed Agarthan Premier League (APL), which has yet to begin operations. Still, sources expect the regulatory authority to spend considerable time explaining why every investigation concludes that everything is perfectly fine.

One proposal would reportedly allow clubs to appeal investigations until the regulator runs out of money, patience or the ability to remember what the investigation was about.

## The Ultimate Sportswashing Project?

The island project could also give wealthy investors an opportunity to improve their public image through football.

The plans reportedly include modern stadiums, elite academies, international tournaments and community projects designed to attract tourists and investors.

Owners could arrive with questionable reputations and leave with honorary awards, club scarves and photographs alongside football legends.

Effstein reportedly believes football can unite people, create jobs and transform cities. He also believes spending the GDP of a small country on a football club every year is a perfectly reasonable way to keep the fans happy.

The league's main objective, meanwhile, is to see which club can outspend everyone else and win everything.

Former politicians, retired regulators and internationally respected consultants could also be invited as honorary advisers. Their main job would be to make the entire operation look respectable on television.

## Every Island Gets a Billionaire

Effstein's ambition is to give every island its own club, stadium, training facilities and billionaire owner.

The clubs would compete for domestic titles, international trophies and the right to spend even more money the following season.

A proposed salary cap was rejected after several owners argued that it would unfairly limit their ability to improve football. A spending limit met a similar fate after investors pointed out that they had already purchased entire islands.

There are even rumours of a transfer market where clubs could pay enormous fees under the table while insisting that their finances remain perfectly sustainable. That way, their success could still be presented as an underdog story.

One investor reportedly asked whether he could spend $400 million on a striker.

The answer was yes, provided the under-the-table payment was submitted correctly and on time.

The striker is believed to be valued at around $90 million by every other club in the world.

## A New Era for Football?

Supporters argue that 115 island cities could create thousands of jobs, attract international investment and establish a new global football destination.

Critics fear a league where the richest owners have almost unlimited influence over clubs, officials and the institutions meant to keep the sport fair.

Effstein has dismissed these concerns, promising transparency, competition and the highest standards of governance. The only condition is that referees allegedly receive payments from state governments to officiate friendly matches in their own countries.

The league has also promised detailed financial records, independent audits and investigations into misconduct. It will, apparently, play as dirty as possible off the pitch.

When asked who would appoint the independent auditors, a spokesperson confirmed that the decision would go to the highest-bidding state owner or billionaire.

And what about filling the stadiums?

Effstein reportedly sees no problem. Why spend money attracting actual fans when you can pay Instagram bots to follow your club and create the appearance of a thriving fanbase?

Theball will continue investigating whether Jeff EffStein is building the future of football or simply constructing 115 islands where every billionaire becomes a football enthusiast, every suspicious sponsorship becomes a commercial revolution and every regulator eventually discovers that the books are perfectly clean.

After all, with enough money, even Financial Fair Play might need a private island of its own.
